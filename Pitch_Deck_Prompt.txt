"The Grid Is Changing — Data Centers Must Adapt"

SUBTITLE (smaller, green):
"Why compute flexibility is no longer optional for new-build GPU data centers"

─── LEFT COLUMN — Three stacked "tension blocks" ───────────────────────────

Block 1 — The Power Crunch  [icon: lightning bolt]
  Headline: "Interconnection queues now run 24–48 months"
  Body: New 200 MW AI data center sites face 2–4 year waits for grid connection
  across ERCOT (Texas), APS/SRP (Arizona), and PJM (Virginia). Utilities are
  overwhelmed by data center demand — standard queue priority goes to the
  lowest-disruption applicants.

Block 2 — The Reliability Paradox  [icon: lock / shield]
  Headline: "Five-nines uptime and grid stability are not opposites"
  Body: GPU workloads are not monolithic. Real-time inference requires
  sub-100ms SLA — but AI pre-training, HPC batch jobs, and research compute
  can tolerate curtailment windows of hours without customer impact.
  Flexibility ≠ unreliability. Flexibility = SLA differentiation.

Block 3 — The Financial Opportunity  [icon: upward arrow chart]
  Headline: "Grid programs pay operators to be flexible"
  Body: ERCOT demand response, PJM capacity markets, and APS/SRP load
  management programs provide direct revenue for load reduction commitments —
  on top of queue acceleration and interconnect cost savings.

  Spike prices $2,500/MWh          ←→ Power cost exposure at scale
  Capacity market obligations      ←→ Revenue diversification beyond GPU rental

Center of diagram: A green band labeled "EMERALD CONDUCTOR" with tagline:
"Turns grid constraints into a competitive moat"

─── BOTTOM STRIP — Three market stats (pill badges, dark cards) ──────────────

SLIDE 2 OF 5 — HOW THE FLEXIBILITY MODEL WORKS
(Audience: Data center operator / infrastructure investor)
Layout: Center-stage framework diagram + supporting mechanics panels
================================================================================

SLIDE TITLE:
"Emerald Conductor: From Workload Intelligence to Grid Value"

SUBTITLE (green):
"A five-step flywheel that transforms compute flexibility into financial returns"

─── CENTER — Circular flywheel diagram (5 steps, clockwise) ─────────────────

Step 1 (top)  — PROFILE  [icon: grid/chip]
  "Map workload mix across flexibility tolerance tiers"
  Default portfolio: 35% Pre-Training · 25% MLOps · 20% Inference ·
                     10% Research · 10% HPC Batch
  Output: Effective DC Flex Capacity = 11.1% of total MW

Step 2 (right) — COMMIT  [icon: handshake]
  "Enroll curtailable MW into DR and capacity programs"
  At 11.1% flex: 22.2 MW per 200 MW site is curtailable
  Minimum commitment threshold: ≥10% for interconnect priority
                                 ≥15% for full queue acceleration
  Output: Utility enrolls site as priority interconnect applicant

Step 3 (bottom-right) — CURTAIL  [icon: power meter]
  "Shift flexible workloads during peak / spike events"
  Texas: 150 spike hours/yr at $2,500/MWh — curtail to avoid exposure
  Virginia: PJM BRA commitment dispatched during peak hours
  Output: Conductor orchestrates job scheduling — zero customer SLA breach

Step 4 (bottom-left) — COLLECT  [icon: dollar sign]
  "Receive DR capacity payments, energy payments, and queue position upgrade"
  Texas: $35/kW-yr capacity + $50/MWh energy during curtailment
  Virginia: $329.17/MW-day × UCAP accreditation × 365 days
  Arizona: $50/kW-yr capacity + $40/MWh energy
  Output: New revenue stream layered on top of GPU rental income

Step 5 (left) — ACCELERATE  [icon: rocket]
  "Flex commitment unlocks earlier interconnection and upsized capacity"
  Texas: 24-month queue → 12-month queue (saving ~$200M time-to-power)
  Virginia: Enables 275 MW connection vs. 200 MW base (75 MW upsize)
  Output: Earlier first-revenue date, larger revenue base

Arrow from Step 5 back to Step 1 with label: "Reinvest in next site"

─── BELOW FLYWHEEL — Key Assumptions Table ──────────────────────────────────

Title: "Model Assumptions — Base Case"

  PARAMETER                   VALUE           NOTE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Portfolio Sites             3               TX/ERCOT, AZ/APS+SRP, VA/PJM
  Total IT Load               600 MW          200 MW per site
  PUE                         1.35            Power Usage Effectiveness
  GPU Revenue Rate            $3.0M/MW/yr     Blended market rate
  Effective Flex %            11.1%           SUMPRODUCT(workload mix × curtailment floor)
  Curtailable MW (per site)   22.2 MW         200 MW × 11.1%
  Queue Acceleration Scale    MIN(1, Flex%/15) Full benefit at ≥15% commitment
  Interconnect Scale          MIN(1, Flex%/10) Full savings at ≥10% commitment
  PJM UCAP Accreditation      90% × flex scale Scales with program commitment depth
  Discount Rate (WACC)        10%
  Analysis Horizon            10 years
  Conductor Platform Cost     $2.0M/yr/site   = $6.0M/yr portfolio total

─── BOTTOM — Formula callout (monospace font, dark code block) ──────────────

  Effective Flex % = SUMPRODUCT(workload_mix%, curtailment_floor%)
                   = (35%×16%) + (25%×8%) + (20%×0%) + (10%×20%) + (10%×15%)
                   = 5.6% + 2.0% + 0.0% + 2.0% + 1.5% = 11.1%

SPEAKER NOTE:
"The 11.1% flex figure is not arbitrary — it's derived bottom-up from the
actual SLA tolerances of each workload type. Inference customers need firm
power. Pre-training customers are actively happy to take a pricing discount
in exchange for occasional curtailment windows. Conductor figures out which
jobs to pause so the operator never breaches a single customer SLA."
